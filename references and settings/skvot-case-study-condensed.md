## Context

SKVOT is Ukraine's largest pop-culture education platform with no mobile experience. As a solo UX/UI designer, I designed a mobile app concept from scratch — from user research to high-fidelity UI.

---

## Problem

The web platform had outdated flows and no mobile app — neither did any competitor. Students relied on Telegram and email to communicate with lecturers. There was no native mobile solution in the market.

---

## Research

15+ qualitative interviews with students and lecturers focused on a specific open question: should the app include homework submission and grading?

**Key findings:**

- **82%** of students submit homework in formats incompatible with mobile (PSD, AI, Figma links)
- **28 of 34** lecturers cannot access homework files from a smartphone — but the majority want grading via mobile

**Research decision:** Homework submission for students doesn't make sense on mobile. Lecturer feedback and grading should be included — the input format (text + grades) works well on mobile.

This finding eliminated a planned feature and redirected scope before a single screen was designed.

---

## Solution

A 4-tab native app shaped entirely by research:

- **Homepage** — Weekly schedule + latest content feed
- **Culture** — Full content library (courses, articles, podcasts, video guides)
- **My Courses** — Enrolled courses with lectures, assignments, grades, and instructor feedback
- **Chat** — Direct student-lecturer-support messaging, replacing Telegram

Competitor analysis confirmed no competitor had a mobile app — giving SKVOT a clear first-mover advantage and validating the 0→1 approach.

---

## Outcome

0→1 concept delivered in 4 weeks. The research-driven decision to cut homework submission for students and keep lecturer grading is the strongest proof of product thinking in this case — building the right thing, not just the obvious thing.

---

## Key Learnings

**Research saves you from building the wrong thing**  
15 minutes of interviews revealed 82% of students can't submit their file formats on mobile. Without research, a near-zero-adoption feature would have shipped.

**First-mover advantage needs a clear scope**  
No competitor had a mobile app. Defining 4 tabs early kept the scope manageable and the experience focused.

**Design for the constraint, not the ideal**  
SKVOT's strict brand guidelines became guardrails, not blockers. The result feels unmistakably SKVOT while being fully optimized for mobile.
